---
title: "Two-factor sign-in"
description: "Add an authenticator app or a passkey to your Darpan account, keep recovery codes, and sign in with a second factor or with a passkey alone."
---

A password alone is one leaked spreadsheet away from someone else reading your tenant's orders. Two-factor closes that gap: after your password, Darpan asks for a code from an app on your phone or a passkey on your device. Setting it up takes about two minutes, and the recovery codes you save at the end mean a lost phone is an inconvenience, not a lockout.

## Before you start

- You can sign in to Darpan with your password.
- One of these:
  - An authenticator app on your phone or computer, such as Google Authenticator, Microsoft Authenticator or 1Password.
  - A passkey or security key: Touch ID or Face ID, Windows Hello, a passkey on your phone, or a key such as a YubiKey. Passkeys appear only when your Darpan instance offers them and your browser can use them.
- Somewhere safe to keep ten recovery codes, such as a password manager.

## Who has to use it

Two-factor is optional until a super admin requires it from a date, for a role or for everyone. The roles are Super admin, Darpan admin, and Tenant admin in any tenant; everyone means every person with a tenant membership or a Darpan role.

- **Before the date:** each sign-in asks **Set up two-factor by** *date*. Choose **Set up**, or **Not now** to carry on; Darpan asks again at the next sign-in.
- **From the date:** a right password shows **Your role needs two-factor. Set it up to sign in.** You set it up there and then, and the sign-in finishes once you have. Nobody is locked out.
- **Reminders:** if you have no app or passkey yet, Darpan emails you when a date is set, again as it nears, and weekly after, never more than one a day. They stop once you set one up.

## Set it up

<Steps>
<Step title="Open User Settings">

Open **Ask Darpan** with `Cmd/Ctrl+K` or by clicking Tara, the red panda in the corner, and search for **Open User Settings**. The **Two-factor** section has an **Authenticator app** card and, when passkeys are offered, a **Passkeys** card.

At a required setup or after **Set up** at sign-in, the same steps open on the sign-in page instead, starting with **Authenticator app or passkey?**.

</Step>
<Step title="Prove it is you">

Choose **Set up** on **Authenticator app**, or **Add** on **Passkeys**. While nothing is on yet, Darpan asks **Enter your current password.** Once a factor is on, every change asks for a code from your app or your passkey instead.

</Step>
<Step title="Add the app or the passkey">

For an authenticator app:

1. **Scan this with your authenticator app.** Scan the QR code. If you cannot, **Can't scan? Key:** shows the key to type in, with **Copy** and **Open in app**.
2. **Enter the code it shows.** Type the 6-digit code; the sixth digit submits it.

For a passkey: **Add a passkey.** Choose **Continue** and follow your browser's prompt.

</Step>
<Step title="Save your recovery codes">

**Save your recovery codes.** shows ten codes. Each works once, in place of a code or passkey. Use **Copy** or **Download**, store them somewhere other than this device, then choose **I saved them**. The codes are shown only this once.

</Step>
</Steps>

## Expected result

The **Authenticator app** card reads **On since** *date* (or **On** when you also have passkeys), each passkey has its own card, and **Recovery codes** shows how many are left. Darpan emails you that an authenticator app was set up or a passkey was added.

Turning two-factor on signs you out everywhere else, CLI sign-ins included. The tab you used stays signed in. Adding a second app or passkey later ends nothing.

## Sign in

After your password, Darpan asks **Enter the code from your app.** Type the current code. To use something else:

- **Use a passkey** asks **Confirm with your passkey.** Choose **Continue** to open your browser's prompt.
- **Use a recovery code** asks **Enter a recovery code.** Each code works once, and Darpan emails you when one is used. When three or fewer remain, the sign-in tells you how many are left.

If you have only passkeys, the passkey step comes first.

### Sign in with a passkey alone

The sign-in page asks **Username?** first. Where passkeys are offered, the username field's autofill lists the passkeys you saved for Darpan: pick one, Touch ID or Face ID confirms it, and you are in, with no password and no code. Once you have signed in with a passkey in this browser, **Next** after your username asks **Use your passkey.** and opens the prompt; **Use password instead** goes to the password. For a security key, or a passkey on another device, choose **Use a passkey or security key** on the password step. The password and a code stay as the fallback.

## Manage your factors

All of these are in **User Settings** under **Two-factor**, and each asks for a current code or your passkey first.

| Card | Action | What it does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Authenticator app** | **New app** | Moves two-factor to a new app. Asks for a code from the current one. |
| **Authenticator app** | **Turn off** | Removes the app. With passkeys left, they stay on; without, two-factor turns off. Not shown when your role requires two-factor and the app is your only factor. |
| **Passkeys** | **Add** | Adds a passkey, up to ten. |
| A passkey | Its name | Select the name to rename it, for example `MacBook Touch ID`. **Added** and **Last used** show its dates. |
| A passkey | **Remove** | Removes that passkey. Your last factor cannot be removed while your role requires two-factor. |
| **Recovery codes** | **New codes** | Replaces every unused code with ten new ones. |

Turning two-factor off signs you out everywhere else, as turning it on does.

## Approve a CLI sign-in

With two-factor on, the **Approve CLI** page asks for **Current code from your app** before **Approve**, or **Use a passkey** (**Approve opens your passkey prompt.**). A code you have just used to sign in is refused for a few seconds; wait for the next one. See [Set up the Darpan CLI](/getting-started/set-up-darpan-cli#sign-in).

## Lost your phone or passkey

Sign in with a recovery code, then use **New app** or **Add** to put a new factor on, and **New codes** to replace the codes you have used. With no recovery code left, ask a Darpan admin to reset your two-factor. A reset removes your app, passkeys and recovery codes and ends your sessions and CLI sign-ins; you set two-factor up again at your next sign-in if your role requires it.
